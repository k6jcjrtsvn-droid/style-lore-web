Color season

Soft Autumn

Warm and muted, with earthy, gently blended coloring.

Soft Autumn is the season most often told it has no season at all. Your hair is a warm mid-tone, dark blonde or light brown with a golden or reddish cast; your eyes are hazel, soft green, warm grey or a gentle brown; and your skin is warm but not obviously golden. Nothing in your face is loud, and the colors that suit you are not loud either.

If a bright coral makes you look like the top is wearing you, but a soft terracotta makes you look rested, you are here. This page gives you the palette, the neutrals, the colors that go wrong, and the answers to the practical questions about black, white and denim that a soft, warm season needs.

The Soft Autumn palette

Six colors that carry this season. They are a direction, not a uniform — the point is the family they belong to, not these exact swatches.

What defines it

UndertoneWarm
DepthMedium
ClarityMuted
ContrastLow

Moderate contrast. You can put a lighter piece against a darker one, but the jump should be a step, not a cliff.

The three dials

Every season is a position on three dials. Hue is undertone: does a color lean toward gold or toward blue. Yours leans warm, but gently: you are the least warm of the autumns, and a color that is warm and soft together will always beat one that is warm and loud. Cool colors, especially icy ones, make you look sallow.

Value is depth. Soft Autumn sits in the middle. You are neither light nor dark, and colors at either extreme, a pale pastel or a near-black, pull attention away from your face rather than toward it.

Chroma is clarity, and it is your defining dial. Soft Autumn sits near the muted end of the whole system. Your colors look as if they have a little grey or a little brown mixed in, and that softness is not a weakness; it is the thing that makes you look expensive in a way brighter seasons cannot. The fourth reading, contrast, is low: keep the pieces close in value and let texture, not light-against-dark, do the work.

Colors that work

Warm and softened — camel, terracotta, olive, soft teal.

Colors that fight it

Icy cool pastels and hard black.

Your best neutrals

Earthy neutrals: camel, olive, chocolate, warm taupe, oatmeal. These are your blacks — an autumn wardrobe built on brown reads more expensive than one built on black.

  • Camel — your best neutral and the color to build a coat wardrobe on.
  • Oatmeal and mushroom — soft, warm off-whites and greiges for knits and shirts.
  • Warm taupe — the mid neutral for trousers and bags.
  • Soft olive and khaki — greyed greens that behave like neutrals.
  • Chocolate-brown and dark olive — the darkest you need. Neither is black, and neither should be.
  • Cream — your white. Never optic.

Your best accents

Your accents are earth colors with the volume turned down a notch. They work as whole outfits and they look best against camel, olive and oatmeal.

  • Terracotta — the signature. A soft, brownish red-orange.
  • Salmon and soft coral — the pinks of your palette, always with peach in them.
  • Soft teal — a greyed blue-green. Your best blue.
  • Muted gold and soft mustard — warm yellows with the brightness taken off.
  • Warm sage and moss — soft yellow-greens.
  • Soft rust and brick rose — the deeper end, for when you want something richer.

Colors to be careful with

Soft Autumn is let down by hardness, whether that is a pure black, an icy pastel or a bright that has no softness in it. The swaps below keep the hue and fix the dial.

  • Black — the most common Soft Autumn mistake. It hollows the face and turns the skin grey. Swap for chocolate, dark olive or a warm charcoal-brown.
  • Optic white — harsh against soft, warm skin. Swap for cream or oatmeal.
  • Icy pastels — powder blue, baby pink, lavender. Swap for peach, soft apricot or warm sage.
  • Hot pink and fuchsia — blue-based and loud. Swap for salmon or brick rose.
  • Electric blue and royal blue — too pure and too cool. Swap for soft teal or a greyed denim blue.
  • Pure tomato red — right family, too much clarity. Swap for terracotta or soft rust.

Metals, denim, black and white

Gold, brass, bronze and copper. Silver tends to look grey and flat against warm skin.

Denim. Faded, warm and soft: a mid-blue with a khaki or brown cast, a washed-out indigo, olive denim. This is one of the few seasons for whom worn-in, faded denim is the flattering choice. Very dark, crisp indigo and black denim look hard on you.

Black. Not near the face, and ideally not at all. Chocolate, dark olive and a warm charcoal-brown do everything black does for you and keep your skin looking alive. If you must wear black, put camel or cream between it and your face.

White. Cream, oatmeal and ivory. Optic white pulls the eye to the shirt and away from you. If a white looks slightly yellow on the hanger next to a bright one, that is your white.

Hair, makeup and glasses notes

Hair. Your natural hair is soft and warm, and the mistake is to make it either much lighter or much cooler. Warm honey, soft golden brown, a caramel or a subtle copper glaze all suit. Ash blonde, platinum and cool black flatten you. When grey arrives, a warm, soft blend suits you better than a stark silver.

Makeup. Lips take warm nude, terracotta, soft brick and a brown-rose. Blush should be a soft peach or terracotta rather than a pink. Brown eyeliner and brown mascara are gentler than black and match the softness of the face; a bronze or taupe eyeshadow suits you better than a grey one.

Glasses. Light tortoiseshell, honey, olive, matte gold and warm taupe frames all sit well. Black frames are heavy on you; silver and clear look cold.

A week of color combinations

Five pairings for a week. Each keeps the values close and the colors soft.

  • Monday — camel trousers, oatmeal knit, terracotta scarf.
  • Tuesday — soft olive with salmon, in roughly equal parts.
  • Wednesday — soft teal with warm taupe, gold at the ears.
  • Thursday — chocolate-brown with muted gold, brown boots.
  • Friday — warm sage with cream, and faded denim.

Most often confused with Soft Summer

Both are gentle and blended, so softness proves nothing. Put camel next to greyed mauve: Soft Autumn comes alive on the camel, Soft Summer looks tired by it.

Neighboring seasons and how to tell

Soft Autumn has one neighbor in its own family and one across the warm-cool line. Both are gentle, so softness will not tell them apart.

True Autumn — Warmer and richer than you. A True Autumn wears full-strength rust and mustard as whole pieces and looks well in them; a Soft Autumn looks slightly overwhelmed by the same colors and better in terracotta and soft mustard. If rust at full strength looks like you, look here.

Soft Summer — Just as soft, but cool. This is the most common mix-up in the system. A Soft Summer wants sage, mauve and dusty blue and looks tired in camel; a Soft Autumn comes alive in camel and looks tired in mauve.

A three-question self-check

Do these in daylight, with a bare face if you can.

  • Hold camel and greyed mauve against your face. If camel wins, you are on the warm side.
  • Hold cream and a soft, cool white. If cream looks fresh and the cool white looks flat, you are an autumn.
  • Wear rust as a whole top. If it looks slightly too much, stay here; if it looks like you, try True Autumn.

Two out of three pointing the same way is enough to start shopping with.

Shopping notes

Search terms surface your colors faster than brand names, and they work in most shops' filters.

  • For neutrals: camel, oatmeal, mushroom, taupe, khaki, olive, cream, chocolate.
  • For accents: terracotta, salmon, soft coral, teal, sage, moss, mustard, rust, clay.
  • Words that usually mean a color is wrong for you: jet black, optic white, ice, neon, electric, hot pink, royal, bright.
  • Fabric: softness shows best in matte, textured fabrics such as brushed cotton, suede, linen, boucle and washed silk; shiny surfaces sharpen a color past your clarity.

The words washed, faded and dusty in a listing are usually good news for you, which is the opposite of what they mean for most other seasons.

Questions people ask

Is Soft Autumn just a boring version of True Autumn?

No. It is a different chroma, not a lesser one. Soft colors make you look expensive and rested; loud colors make you look as if the clothes are wearing you. The palette is quieter, but it is yours.

Can I wear black at all?

Away from the face, in trousers or shoes, yes. As a top or a coat collar it drains you. Chocolate, dark olive and warm charcoal-brown are the darks to look for.

Gold or silver?

Gold, but soft gold: brushed, antique or matte rather than bright polished. Bronze and copper also suit. Silver reads cold and slightly grey against warm, soft skin.

What if the quiz said Soft Summer?

The two are neighbors and the difference is undertone only. Do the camel-against-mauve test above in daylight. Whichever color makes your skin look alive rather than tired is your side of the line.
