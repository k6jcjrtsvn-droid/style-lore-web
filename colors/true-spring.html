Color season

True Spring

Warm and clear, with medium depth and a bright, golden glow.

True Spring is the season that looks lit from inside. Your hair is warm and golden, whether honey blonde, copper, strawberry or a golden mid-brown; your eyes are often clear blue, green, turquoise or a warm topaz brown; and your skin has a golden or peachy glow that tans easily and freckles readily.

If a dusty rose makes you look faded and a clear coral makes you look well, you are here. You are the center of the spring family: warm without being dark, clear without being loud. This page gives you the palette, the neutrals, the colors to be careful with, and honest answers about black, white and denim.

The True Spring palette

Six colors that carry this season. They are a direction, not a uniform — the point is the family they belong to, not these exact swatches.

What defines it

UndertoneWarm
DepthMedium
ClarityClear
ContrastMedium

Moderate contrast. You can put a lighter piece against a darker one, but the jump should be a step, not a cliff.

The three dials

Every season is a position on three dials. Hue is undertone: does a color lean toward gold or toward blue. Yours sits firmly at the gold end, the warmest position in the system alongside True Autumn. Every color that suits you has yellow inside it, and colors with blue inside them, even bright ones, look borrowed.

Value is depth. True Spring sits in the middle: deeper than a Light Spring, without the darkness that lets a Bright Spring flirt with black. Mid-tone warm colors worn as whole outfits look most like you, and a bright navy or a golden brown is as dark as you need to go.

Chroma is clarity. Yours is clear, though not the pure pigment of a Bright Spring: your colors look fresh and sunlit, like a garden after rain, with no grey and no brown stirred in. A dusty peach is too flat; a neon coral is slightly too sharp; a clear coral is exactly right. The fourth reading, contrast, is medium: a lighter piece against a darker one works, but a stark black-and-white split looks harsher than you are.

Colors that work

Warm and clear at full clarity — golden yellow, coral, turquoise, grass green.

Colors that fight it

Dusty or greyed-off shades, and cool blue-based darks.

Your best neutrals

Warm light neutrals: ivory, camel, warm beige, light denim, soft gold-brown. Skip pure white and black — both are harsher than anything in your own coloring.

  • Camel — your best neutral, warm and mid-depth.
  • Ivory and warm white — your whites. Softer and warmer than optic white.
  • Golden brown — the darkest brown you need, closer to toffee than to chocolate.
  • Bright navy — a navy with a little green or a little brightness in it. Your alternative to black.
  • Warm beige and khaki-gold — mid neutrals for trousers and jackets.
  • Light golden grey — a grey that leans yellow rather than blue, if you want a grey at all.

Your best accents

Your accents are the clear warm colors at full freshness. They work as whole outfits and look best against camel, ivory and bright navy.

  • Coral — the signature. A clear, warm pink-orange.
  • Golden yellow — bright, warm and sunny.
  • Turquoise — your blue, always with green in it.
  • Grass and leaf green — yellow-based greens, kept clear.
  • Warm orange and tangerine — the oranges of your family, worn confidently.
  • Tomato and poppy red — reds with orange underneath, never blue.

Colors to be careful with

True Spring is let down by dullness first and by coolness second. The swaps below keep the hue and fix the dial.

  • Dusty rose and mauve — greyed and cool. They make you look faded. Swap for coral or warm pink.
  • Black and charcoal — too heavy and too cool near the face. Swap for bright navy or golden brown.
  • Burgundy and plum — cool, blue-based darks. Swap for tomato red.
  • Olive and mustard — warm but muted; they belong to Autumn. Swap for leaf green and golden yellow.
  • Cool grey and icy pastels — draining. Swap for warm beige, ivory or peach.
  • Optic white — slightly too blue. Swap for ivory.

Metals, denim, black and white

Gold, brass, bronze and copper. Silver tends to look grey and flat against warm skin.

Denim. Mid-blue washes with a warm, clean cast are yours, and a bright, clear indigo also works. Ecru and cream denim suit you. Grey-cast and black denim look cold near the face, and heavily faded yellow washes look muddy.

Black. Not near the face. Black trousers and shoes are fine because they sit far from your skin; a black top or coat collar drains the glow that is your best feature. Bright navy and golden brown give you a dark that works with your warmth.

White. Ivory and warm white. Optic white is slightly too blue and makes the skin look pink rather than golden. If a white looks faintly creamy next to a bright one on the hanger, that is yours.

Hair, makeup and glasses notes

Hair. Your hair is your glow, and the mistake is to cool it. Golden blonde, honey, copper, strawberry and a warm golden brown all suit; ash tones make your skin look grey and platinum looks harsh. If you color, add warmth and light rather than depth. When grey arrives, a warm, golden blend suits you better than a stark silver.

Makeup. Lips take coral, peach, warm pink and a clear tomato red. Blush should be a peach or a warm coral. Brown, bronze and warm navy eyeliner suit you better than black, and a brown mascara is worth trying. Mauve lips and grey eyeshadow are the things to avoid.

Glasses. Honey tortoiseshell, gold, amber, clear and warm brown frames all sit well. Black frames are heavy on you; silver looks cold.

A week of color combinations

Five pairings for a week. Each keeps a warm, clear color near the face and the contrast moderate.

  • Monday — camel trousers, ivory shirt, coral knit.
  • Tuesday — bright navy with golden yellow, gold at the ears.
  • Wednesday — turquoise with warm beige, in roughly equal parts.
  • Thursday — leaf green with golden brown, tan shoes.
  • Friday — tomato red with mid-blue denim, and an ivory scarf.

Most often confused with True Autumn

Both are warm, and that is the trap. True Spring is clear — the color looks like it has a light behind it. True Autumn is the same warmth with the light turned down and earth mixed in.

Neighboring seasons and how to tell

True Spring has neighbors on both sides in its own family. Both are warm, so warmth will not tell them apart. Depth and clarity will.

Light Spring — Warm but lighter and softer than you. A Light Spring looks best in peach and apricot and finds a full coral or a grass green slightly overwhelming. If the pale versions of your colors look more like you than the full-strength ones, look here.

Bright Spring — Warm but louder and higher in contrast than you. A Bright Spring wants poppy red and hot coral at maximum saturation and a real light-dark gap in every outfit; a True Spring looks slightly overwhelmed by both. If the loudest version of a color always wins on you, look here.

A three-question self-check

Do these in daylight, with a bare face if you can.

  • Wear a clear coral as a whole top. If it looks like you, you are a True Spring. If it looks too much, try Light Spring; if you want it louder, try Bright Spring.
  • Hold ivory and optic white against your jaw. If ivory is clean and optic white looks blue, you are on the warm side.
  • Hold camel and black. If camel looks complete and black looks harsh, stay in the spring family.

Two out of three pointing the same way is enough to start shopping with.

Shopping notes

Search terms surface your colors faster than brand names, and they work in most shops' filters.

  • For neutrals: camel, ivory, warm white, toffee, golden brown, bright navy, khaki.
  • For accents: coral, golden yellow, turquoise, grass green, leaf green, tangerine, tomato red, poppy.
  • Words that usually mean a color is wrong for you: dusty, heather, jet black, charcoal, burgundy, olive, mustard, ice.
  • Fabric: clarity shows best in smooth, light-reflecting fabrics such as cotton poplin, sateen, fine knit, silk and clean denim; fuzzy, brushed or slubby textures grey a color past your clarity.

When a listing offers a color in both a warm and a cool version, such as coral and rose, the warm one is yours.

Questions people ask

Can a True Spring wear black?

Below the waist and away from the face, yes. As a top or a coat collar it drains your glow. Bright navy and golden brown are the darks to look for, and they look more alive on you than black does.

Is there a red for me?

Yes: tomato, poppy and a clear orange-red. The reds to avoid are the ones with blue in them, cherry and burgundy. If a red looks slightly orange, it is probably yours.

Gold or silver?

Gold, and bright, polished gold rather than antique. Rose gold also suits. Silver looks slightly grey and flat against warm, golden skin.

What if the quiz said True Autumn?

Both are warm, and that is the trap. The difference is clarity: True Spring colors look as though there is a light behind them; True Autumn colors are the same warmth with the light turned down and earth mixed in. Hold coral against rust in daylight. Whichever makes your face look fresh rather than heavy is your answer.
