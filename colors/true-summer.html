Color season

True Summer

Cool and muted, with medium depth and a gentle contrast.

True Summer is the season of cool, gentle mid-tones: the rose that is not quite pink, the navy that is not quite black, the blue-grey of a sea on an overcast day. Your hair is cool, whether ash blonde, ash brown or a soft cool brunette; your eyes are often grey-blue, blue, soft green or a cool hazel; and your skin has a pink or blue cast that flushes rather than tans.

If a bright orange makes you look unwell and a soft raspberry makes you look rested, you are here. This page gives you the palette, the neutrals, the colors that go wrong on you, and honest answers about black, white and denim for a season that is cool without being cold.

The True Summer palette

Six colors that carry this season. They are a direction, not a uniform — the point is the family they belong to, not these exact swatches.

What defines it

UndertoneCool

ContrastMedium

Moderate contrast. You can put a lighter piece against a darker one, but the jump should be a step, not a cliff.

The three dials

Every season is a position on three dials. Hue is undertone: does a color lean toward gold or toward blue. Yours sits firmly at the cool end, the coolest position in the system alongside True Winter. Every color that suits you has blue inside it, and colors with yellow or orange inside them, even the soft ones, make your skin look sallow.

Value is depth. True Summer sits in the middle: darker than a Light Summer, without the greyed depth that a Soft Summer can carry. Mid-tone colors worn as whole outfits look most like you, and a soft navy is as dark as you need to go.

Chroma is clarity. Yours is muted, though not as muted as Soft Summer: your colors look as though they have a fine mist over them rather than a fog. A pure fuchsia is too sharp; a heavily greyed mauve is slightly too flat; a clear rose is exactly right. The fourth reading, contrast, is medium: a lighter piece against a darker one works, but a hard black-and-white split looks harsher than you are.

Colors that work

Cool and gently greyed — rose, teal, soft navy, plum.

Colors that fight it

Orange, warm golds, neon, and stark black-against-white.

Your best neutrals

Cool soft neutrals: soft navy, greyed taupe, oyster, cocoa with a rose cast, and a grey that leans blue rather than yellow. Soft white beats stark white.

  • Soft navy — your darkest neutral and your best one. Slightly greyed rather than inky.
  • Slate and blue-grey — mid greys with blue in them, for suiting and coats.
  • Rose-taupe — a taupe with a pink cast rather than a yellow one.
  • Cocoa with a rose cast — your brown. Never a golden or reddish brown.
  • Soft white — your white. Cooler than cream, softer than optic.
  • Dove grey — the light neutral for shirts and knits.

Your best accents

Your accents are the cool colors at a gentle volume. They work as whole outfits and look best against soft navy, slate and soft white.

  • Rose and raspberry — the signature pinks: cool, clear enough to read, never hot.
  • Teal and spruce — blue-greens with more blue than green.
  • Plum — a cool, mid-depth purple.
  • Periwinkle and lavender — soft blue-purples.
  • Watermelon — your red: a cool, pinkish red rather than a tomato.
  • Denim blue — the mid blue of a good pair of jeans, worn as a color in its own right.

Colors to be careful with

True Summer is let down by warmth first and by hardness second. The swaps below keep the hue and fix the dial.

  • Orange, rust and coral — the fastest way to look sallow. Swap for raspberry or watermelon.
  • Warm gold and mustard — they turn the skin yellow. Swap for a pale, cool lemon in small amounts, or drop yellow entirely.
  • Black — too hard, especially near the face. Swap for soft navy or charcoal.
  • Optic white — too stark against soft skin. Swap for soft white.
  • Camel and warm beige — warm and dulling. Swap for rose-taupe or dove grey.
  • Neon and electric brights — too pure for your clarity. Swap for teal, plum or a clear rose.

Metals, denim, black and white

Silver, platinum and white gold. Yellow gold usually reads slightly dirty against cool skin.

Denim. Cool mid-blue and a soft, greyed dark blue are yours, and denim blue is an accent as well as a neutral for you. A grey-cast wash is good; a yellowed, warm wash is not. Black denim is fine away from the face.

Black. Not near the face. A black top or coat collar makes your skin look grey and shows every shadow under the eyes. Soft navy and charcoal give you the same seriousness and look more expensive on you. Black trousers and shoes are fine.

White. Soft white, the white of a good linen sheet rather than a new printer page. Cream and ivory turn you yellow; optic white makes the face look pale by comparison. If in doubt, choose the white that looks slightly greyer on the hanger.

Hair, makeup and glasses notes

Hair. Your natural hair is cool, and the mistake is to warm it. Ash blonde, cool beige-blonde, ash brown and a smoky brunette all suit; golden highlights, caramel balayage and copper all pull your skin sallow. If you want to go lighter, keep it ashy. When grey arrives, a clean silver suits you.

Makeup. Lips take rose, raspberry, cool berry and a rose-nude. Blush should be a cool pink rather than a peach. Grey, navy and plum eyeliner are gentler than black and suit the softness of your face; brown-black mascara sits better than hard black. Warm bronzers are the thing to avoid.

Glasses. Silver, gunmetal, blue-grey, plum and a cool, greyed tortoiseshell all sit well. Gold and honey tortoise look warm and heavy; black frames can work if they are thin.

A week of color combinations

Five pairings for a week. Each keeps the contrast moderate and the colors cool.

  • Monday — soft navy trousers, soft white shirt, raspberry knit.
  • Tuesday — slate grey with rose, silver at the ears.
  • Wednesday — teal with rose-taupe, in roughly equal parts.
  • Thursday — plum with dove grey, and cool mid-blue denim.
  • Friday — periwinkle with soft navy, grey shoes.

Most often confused with Soft Summer

Both cool and both muted. True Summer can still carry a color that reads clearly — a real rose, a real teal. Soft Summer needs everything greyed a step further before it settles.

Neighboring seasons and how to tell

True Summer has neighbors on both sides in its own family. Both are cool, so undertone will not tell them apart. Depth and clarity will.

Light Summer — Cool but lighter and more delicate than you. A Light Summer looks best in the palest version of every color and finds a mid-depth plum or teal slightly heavy. If your best colors are the pale ones, look here.

Soft Summer — Cool but softer than you. A Soft Summer needs a clear rose greyed one step further before it settles, and can carry a hint of warmth that would turn you sallow. If a clear rose pink looks slightly too much on you, look here.

A three-question self-check

Do these in daylight, with a bare face if you can.

  • Wear a clear rose pink as a whole top. If it looks like you, you are a True Summer. If it looks too much, try Soft Summer; if it looks heavy, try Light Summer.
  • Hold soft white and cream against your jaw. If soft white looks fresh and cream looks yellow, you are a summer.
  • Hold soft navy and black. If navy looks calm and black looks harsh, stay in the summer family.

Two out of three pointing the same way is enough to start shopping with.

Shopping notes

Search terms surface your colors faster than brand names, and they work in most shops' filters.

  • For neutrals: navy, slate, blue-grey, dove grey, rose-taupe, cocoa, soft white.
  • For accents: rose, raspberry, teal, spruce, plum, periwinkle, lavender, watermelon, denim blue.
  • Words that usually mean a color is wrong for you: rust, coral, mustard, camel, gold, neon, jet black, optic white.
  • Fabric: cool softness shows best in matte cottons, fine wool, washed silk and soft jersey; very shiny surfaces sharpen a color past your clarity, and very fuzzy ones grey it past it.

When a listing offers a color in both warm and cool versions, such as blush and rose, the cool one, rose, is yours.

Questions people ask

Can a True Summer wear black?

Below the waist and away from the face, yes. As a top or a coat collar it greys the skin. Soft navy and charcoal are the darks to look for, and they look calmer and more expensive on you.

Is there any red for me?

Yes: watermelon, raspberry and a cool cherry. The reds to avoid are the ones with orange in them, tomato and brick. If a red looks slightly pink, it is probably yours.

Gold or silver?

Silver, platinum, white gold and pewter. A cool, pale rose gold can work. Yellow gold reads warm and slightly dirty against cool skin, especially near the face.

What if the quiz said Soft Summer or Light Summer?

All three are cool, so the question is depth and clarity, not undertone. Try the three-question check above in daylight. If mid-tone, clear-ish colors like a real rose or a real teal look best, you are a True Summer.
