Color season

Deep Autumn

Warm and deep, with rich, high-contrast coloring.

Deep Autumn is the warm dark: dark brown or black-brown hair, eyes that are deep brown, hazel or dark green, and skin with a golden or olive cast at any depth from light to very deep. If chocolate brown makes you look expensive and pale pink makes you look ill, this is your page.

You share depth with Deep Winter and warmth with True Autumn, and you can borrow from both, but your own palette is the rich, dark end of the earth colors. What follows is the palette, the neutrals worth investing in, the colors that let you down, and honest answers about black, denim and gold.

The Deep Autumn palette

Six colors that carry this season. They are a direction, not a uniform — the point is the family they belong to, not these exact swatches.

What defines it

UndertoneWarm
DepthDeep
ClarityMedium
ContrastHigh

Let the pieces contrast. A light piece against a dark one matches the contrast already in your own coloring; an all-mid-tone outfit can look like it has gone slightly grey.

The three dials

Every season is a position on three dials. Hue is undertone: does a color lean toward gold or toward blue. Yours leans warm, though not as far as a True Autumn, which is why the cooler darks, a blue-black or a deep pine, do not hurt you the way an icy pastel does. The word to remember is rich: warm colors with weight behind them.

Value is depth, and it is your defining dial. Deep Autumn sits at the dark end of the whole system. Your best colors are as dark as your hair, and lighter colors need to be either very warm or kept away from the face.

Chroma is clarity, and yours is medium: not the greyed softness of a Soft Autumn, not the pure pigment of a Bright Winter. Your colors are saturated but never sharp, the way a good leather or a glass of red wine is saturated. The fourth reading, contrast, is high, because your dark hair sits far from your skin and eye-whites, so an outfit that pairs a deep color with a warm light one looks like you.

Colors that work

Deep warm darks — chocolate, forest, burgundy, bronze — with real contrast between pieces.

Colors that fight it

Light dusty pastels, which wash out against this depth.

Your best neutrals

Earthy neutrals: camel, olive, chocolate, warm taupe, oatmeal. These are your blacks — an autumn wardrobe built on brown reads more expensive than one built on black.

  • Chocolate — the darkest, richest brown. This is your black, and it looks better on you than black does.
  • Black-brown and espresso — for coats and boots, the near-black that still has warmth in it.
  • Forest and deep olive — dark greens that function as neutrals. A forest coat is a Deep Autumn signature.
  • Dark teal — deep, warm blue-green. Your alternative to navy.
  • Camel and bronze — your mid neutrals, for trousers, knits and bags.
  • Cream and oatmeal — the light neutrals. Warm off-white rather than pure white.

Your best accents

Your accents are the rich end of the earth colors. They can be worn as whole outfits, and they look best against chocolate, forest or cream.

  • Burgundy and oxblood — a brown-based wine, deep and warm.
  • Tomato and brick red — reds with orange underneath, at full depth.
  • Mustard and antique gold — the deep, warm yellows.
  • Rust and terracotta — the oranges of your family, worn dark.
  • Deep teal and peacock — saturated blue-greens with warmth in them.
  • Aubergine — a warm, brownish purple. The only purple that is truly yours.

Colors to be careful with

Deep Autumn fails in two directions: pale and dusty, which wash out against your depth, and icy and cool, which turn your skin sallow. The swaps below keep the hue and fix the dial.

  • Pastel pink, powder blue and lavender — they disappear against your hair and make the skin look grey. Swap for cream, or drop to the deep version of the hue: burgundy, teal, aubergine.
  • Cool grey and silver-grey — flat and cold on you. Swap for chocolate, espresso or dark olive.
  • Icy pink and fuchsia — blue-based pinks read harsh. Swap for brick red or a warm rose-brown.
  • Pure optic white — it makes you look tired. Swap for cream or oatmeal.
  • Neon and acid brights — too sharp for your medium clarity. Swap for a saturated but deep version: peacock rather than turquoise, tomato rather than poppy.
  • Mid-tone dusty shades — soft sage, dusty rose, greige. They belong to your soft neighbors. Swap for olive, rust or bronze.

Metals, denim, black and white

Gold, brass, bronze and copper. Silver tends to look grey and flat against warm skin.

Denim. Dark indigo with a warm, slightly brown cast is your denim; black-brown and dark olive denim also work. Mid and light washes look faded on you unless they are worn with something deep near the face.

Black. You are the autumn that gets away with it most often. Black works as trousers, boots and a coat, and it can be worn near the face if a warm rich color sits between it and your skin. Chocolate is still better, and if you are choosing one dark to invest in, choose that.

White. Cream, ivory and oatmeal, not pure white. A cream shirt under a chocolate jacket is one of your best combinations; an optic white shirt in the same place flattens the face.

Hair, makeup and glasses notes

Hair. Your natural hair is dark and warm: espresso, dark chestnut, deep auburn, black-brown. If you color, keep the depth and add warmth rather than lightness: chestnut, mahogany, deep copper lowlights. Ash tones and cool black flatten you, and going more than a shade lighter softens the season away.

Makeup. Lips take brick, deep terracotta, warm berry and oxblood. Blush should be a warm rose-brown or terracotta rather than a cool pink. Brown, bronze and deep plum eyeliner suit you better than black, and a warm brown mascara is worth trying. Very pale or frosted lips look wrong.

Glasses. Dark tortoiseshell, bronze, chocolate, deep olive and matte gold all sit well. Black works if the frame is thin. Silver and clear frames look cold beside your skin.

A week of color combinations

Five pairings for a week. Each keeps a deep warm color close to the face.

  • Monday — chocolate trousers, cream shirt, burgundy knit or scarf.
  • Tuesday — forest green with camel: a forest coat over camel.
  • Wednesday — dark teal with rust, in equal parts.
  • Thursday — black trousers, mustard top, bronze at the ears.
  • Friday — aubergine with oatmeal, and brown boots.

Most often confused with Deep Winter

Both go dark and both take contrast. Deep Autumn's darks have brown and bronze in them; Deep Winter's have blue and black. Compare chocolate against true black — one of them will look like it belongs on you.

Neighboring seasons and how to tell

Deep Autumn has one neighbor in its own family and one across the warm-cool line. Both share your depth, so depth will not tell them apart.

True Autumn — Warmer and a step lighter than you. A True Autumn is at its best in mid-depth earth colors, rust and mustard and olive, and does not need the darkest browns. If chocolate and burgundy make you look richer than rust does, stay here; if rust is the one that lights you up, look next door.

Deep Winter — Just as dark, but cool. A Deep Winter looks alive in true black and pure white and drained by camel; a Deep Autumn is the reverse. Compare chocolate with black against your face, and cream with pure white.

A three-question self-check

Do these in daylight, with a bare face if you can.

  • Hold chocolate and black against your face. If chocolate is clearly better, you are an autumn.
  • Hold cream and pure white. If cream is clean and white is harsh, you are an autumn.
  • Wear rust as a whole top. If it looks as good as burgundy does, you may be a True Autumn; if burgundy wins, stay here.

Two out of three pointing the same way is enough to start shopping with.

Shopping notes

Search terms surface your colors faster than brand names, and they work in most shops' filters.

  • For neutrals: chocolate, espresso, dark olive, forest green, dark teal, camel, cream, oatmeal.
  • For accents: burgundy, oxblood, brick red, rust, mustard, antique gold, peacock, aubergine.
  • Words that usually mean a color is wrong for you: pastel, powder, ice, icy, silver, optic white, neon, dusty.
  • Fabric: depth and warmth show best in leather, suede, velvet, wool, corduroy and heavy knits; shiny synthetic surfaces can make a warm dark look plasticky.

When a product is offered in brown and black, the brown is almost always the right call for you.

Questions people ask

Can a Deep Autumn wear black?

More easily than any other autumn, yes. Keep it away from the face or put a rich warm color between black and your skin. Chocolate does everything black does for you and does it better.

Gold or silver?

Gold, bronze, brass and copper. Antique or brushed gold suits you better than very bright polished gold. Silver reads cold and slightly cheap against warm, deep skin.

I have very dark skin. Is Deep Autumn still about warmth?

Yes. The season describes undertone, depth and clarity, and deep brown skin with a golden or red cast is one of the most common Deep Autumn combinations. Mustard, burgundy, forest and rust look extraordinary on it.

The quiz said Deep Autumn but rust looks better on me than chocolate. Why?

You may sit close to True Autumn, which is the same warmth at a lighter depth. Read that page and try the three-question check above; if rust and mustard consistently beat the darkest browns, that is your answer.
