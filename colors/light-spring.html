Color season

Light Spring

Warm and light, with soft rather than intense coloring.

Light Spring is the season of the first warm day of the year: peach, apricot, the yellow of primroses. Your hair is light and warm, whether golden blonde, strawberry blonde or a light golden brown; your eyes are often blue, green, aqua or a light hazel; and your skin is fair with a peachy or golden cast that freckles.

If a black turtleneck makes you look tired and a peach one makes you look as though you have been on holiday, you are here. This page gives you the palette, the neutrals, the colors to be careful with, and honest answers about black, white and denim for a season that is easily overpowered.

The Light Spring palette

Six colors that carry this season. They are a direction, not a uniform — the point is the family they belong to, not these exact swatches.

What defines it

UndertoneWarm
DepthLight
ClarityMedium
ContrastLow

Keep the values close. A low-contrast outfit — one family of tones head to toe — reads as intentional on you, while a hard light-against-dark split looks like the clothes are wearing you.

The three dials

Every season is a position on three dials. Hue is undertone: does a color lean toward gold or toward blue. Yours leans warm, though gently: a pale, cool-leaning aqua or a soft periwinkle will not hurt you, but a cool pink or an icy grey will make you look sallow. Peach, not pink, is the test.

Value is depth, and it is your defining dial. Light Spring sits at the light end of the whole system. Your hair, skin and eyes are all pale, and a color much darker than any of them pulls the eye off your face.

Chroma is clarity. Yours is medium: clearer than a Light Summer, softer than a True Spring. Your colors are fresh rather than bright, like fruit sorbet rather than boiled sweets. A hot coral is too loud; a dusty peach is slightly too flat; a clear, pale apricot is exactly right. The fourth reading, contrast, is low: keep the values close and let the whole outfit sit in the light-to-mid range.

Colors that work

Warm and light — peach, coral, soft aqua, buttery yellow. Keep the values close together; nothing very deep.

Colors that fight it

Black, charcoal, heavy jewel tones, and anything cool and icy.

Your best neutrals

Warm light neutrals: ivory, camel, warm beige, light denim, soft gold-brown. Skip pure white and black — both are harsher than anything in your own coloring.

  • Ivory and cream — your whites, warm and soft. Never optic.
  • Light camel and sand — the beiges of your palette, pale and golden.
  • Warm light grey — a grey with a hint of yellow rather than blue, for trousers and knits.
  • Light warm navy — the darkest you need: a soft, slightly greened navy rather than ink.
  • Light golden brown — your brown, closer to toffee than to chocolate.
  • Light denim — a neutral you can wear in quantity.

Your best accents

Your accents are the pale warm colors, worn as whole pieces so they do not vanish. They look best against ivory, sand and light denim.

  • Peach and apricot — the signature. Soft, warm and clear.
  • Coral, kept light — a pale coral or a salmon rather than a hot one.
  • Soft aqua and light turquoise — your blues, always with green in them.
  • Buttery yellow — pale, warm and clear.
  • Apple green and light leaf green — yellow-based greens, kept fresh.
  • Warm pink — a pink with peach in it, for when you want pink.

Colors to be careful with

Light Spring is let down by weight first and by coolness second. The swaps below keep the hue and fix the dial.

  • Black and charcoal — they swallow the face and flatten the hair. Swap for light warm navy or light golden brown.
  • Cool icy pastels — icy pink, icy blue, lavender-grey. They make your skin look sallow. Swap for peach, soft aqua or buttery yellow.
  • Burgundy and other deep jewel tones — too heavy against light coloring. Swap for coral or warm pink.
  • Cool grey — cold and draining. Swap for warm light grey or sand.
  • Optic white — slightly too stark. Swap for ivory.
  • Olive, mustard and rust — right side of the wheel, far too heavy and too muted. Swap for apple green, buttery yellow or light coral.

Metals, denim, black and white

Gold, brass, bronze and copper. Silver tends to look grey and flat against warm skin.

Denim. Light and mid-blue washes with a warm, slightly faded cast are yours, and you can wear a lot of them. Ecru and cream denim also work. Very dark indigo and black denim look heavy near the face; grey-cast washes look cold.

Black. No, and this is one of the seasons where the rule really holds. Black near the face makes you look drained and makes light hair look flat. Light warm navy, light golden brown and warm mid grey all give you a dark to work with. If black is required, keep it below the waist and put ivory or peach between it and your face.

White. Ivory and cream. Optic white is slightly too bright and too blue; it makes fair skin look pink. If a white looks faintly yellow next to a bright one on the hanger, that is yours.

Hair, makeup and glasses notes

Hair. Your hair is light and warm, and the mistake is to cool it. Golden blonde, honey, strawberry and a light warm brown all suit; ash and platinum tones make your skin look grey. If your hair has darkened with age, warm golden highlights that bring it back toward its childhood color usually flatter. When grey arrives, a warm, creamy blend suits you better than a stark silver.

Makeup. Keep it light and warm. Lips take peach, light coral and a warm pink-nude; blush should be a soft peach or apricot. Brown eyeliner and brown mascara are gentler than black and suit the lightness of your face. Cool pink lips and grey eyeshadow are the things to avoid.

Glasses. Light tortoiseshell, honey, gold, clear and pale peach frames all sit well. Black frames dominate a light face; silver looks cold.

A week of color combinations

Five pairings for a week. Each keeps the values light and close together.

  • Monday — sand trousers, ivory shirt, peach knit.
  • Tuesday — soft aqua with light camel, gold at the ears.
  • Wednesday — buttery yellow with light denim, in roughly equal parts.
  • Thursday — light coral with warm light grey, tan shoes.
  • Friday — apple green with light warm navy, and an ivory scarf.

Most often confused with Light Summer

Both are light and soft, so the depth is no help. The undertone is. Hold peach against soft pink: Light Spring warms up next to the peach, Light Summer goes sallow and needs the pink.

Neighboring seasons and how to tell

Light Spring has one neighbor in its own family and one across the warm-cool line. Both are gentle, so gentleness will not tell them apart.

True Spring — Warm but deeper and clearer than you. A True Spring can wear a full coral, a grass green or a golden yellow as a whole piece and look finished; a Light Spring looks slightly overwhelmed by the same colors. If the full-strength versions look like you and the pale ones look faint, look here.

Light Summer — Just as light, but cool. A Light Summer glows in powder blue and rose pink and looks sallow in peach; a Light Spring is the reverse. If gold looks warm and clean on you and silver looks slightly grey, you are a spring.

A three-question self-check

Do these in daylight, with a bare face if you can.

  • Hold peach and rose pink against your face. If peach wins, you are on the warm side.
  • Hold ivory and soft cool white. If ivory looks fresh and the cool white looks stark, you are a spring.
  • Wear a full-strength coral as a whole top. If it looks too loud, stay here; if it looks like you, try True Spring.

Two out of three pointing the same way is enough to start shopping with.

Shopping notes

Search terms surface your colors faster than brand names, and they work in most shops' filters.

  • For neutrals: ivory, cream, sand, light camel, ecru, toffee, light navy.
  • For accents: peach, apricot, light coral, salmon, aqua, light turquoise, butter yellow, apple green.
  • Words that usually mean a color is wrong for you: jet black, charcoal, ice, icy, optic white, burgundy, olive, rust.
  • Fabric: lightness shows best in fine, soft fabrics such as cotton voile, fine knit, washed linen, chambray and light jersey; heavy, dense or very dark-dyed fabrics add weight a light season does not have.

The words pale, light, sorbet and pastel in a listing are usually good news for you, provided the base color is warm.

Questions people ask

Is Light Spring just pastels?

The palette is pale, but not only pastel. Light warm navy, light golden brown and a full coral give you depth when you need it. The rule is warm and light, not warm and faint.

Can I wear black to a formal event?

Choose light warm navy, warm mid grey or a light golden brown instead; they read just as formal and keep your face alive. If black is required, wear it below the waist and put ivory or peach at the neck.

Gold or silver?

Gold, and light, bright gold rather than antique. Rose gold also suits. Silver looks slightly grey and flat against warm, fair skin. Small, delicate pieces match the scale of your coloring.

What if the quiz said Light Summer?

The two are neighbors and the difference is undertone only. Do the peach-against-rose test above in daylight. Whichever makes your skin look alive rather than sallow is your side of the line.
