Color season

Deep Winter

Cool and deep, with dramatic, high-contrast coloring.

Deep Winter is the season of the dramatic dark: black hair or near-black hair, dark eyes, and skin that can be anything from pale to deep brown but always carries a cool or neutral cast. If a camel coat makes you look faded and a black one makes you look expensive, you are probably here.

What sets you apart from the other winters is depth. Your darkest colors are the ones that look most like you, and light colors have to earn their place by being very clean or very pale. This page covers the palette, the neutrals, the colors that go wrong on you and the practical questions about black, denim and metals.

The Deep Winter palette

Six colors that carry this season. They are a direction, not a uniform — the point is the family they belong to, not these exact swatches.

What defines it

UndertoneCool
DepthDeep
ClarityMedium
ContrastHigh

Let the pieces contrast. A light piece against a dark one matches the contrast already in your own coloring; an all-mid-tone outfit can look like it has gone slightly grey.

The three dials

Every season is a position on three dials. Hue is undertone: does a color lean toward gold or toward blue. Yours leans cool, but not as sharply as a True Winter, which is why the neutral-cool darks such as a very dark chocolate or a cool burgundy can sit near your face without harm. Warm and light together is what fails you.

Value is depth, and it is your defining dial. Deep Winter sits near the dark end of the whole system. Your hair and often your eyes are close to black, and colors that match that depth look like part of you; colors that are pale and dusty look like a mistake.

Chroma is clarity, how pure a color is. Yours is medium: clearer than any Autumn, softer than a Bright Winter. That is why you can wear both a pure true red and a rich, slightly deeper pine green. The fourth reading, contrast, is high because your dark hair sits far from the whites of your eyes, so a dark piece against a light or bright one repeats what your face is already doing.

Colors that work

Cool darks with a clear bright beside them — black, true red, emerald, deep navy, icy pink.

Colors that fight it

Muted earth tones, camel, warm beige.

Your best neutrals

Stark neutrals: true black, charcoal, pure white, deep navy. Winter is the one family that can wear real black next to real white and look better for it.

  • True black — the best neutral in the system for you. Wear it anywhere, including head to toe.
  • Pure white — as bright and blue-white as you can find. Its job is to stand against black.
  • Charcoal — for the days black feels heavy, especially in soft fabrics.
  • Deep navy — an inky, almost-black navy. Reads as softer than black without being warm.
  • Pewter grey — a dark, cool mid-grey for suiting and knitwear.
  • Black-brown — the darkest, coolest espresso, worn with white or a bright. Your one concession to brown.

Your best accents

Your accents are jewel tones and their icy opposites. The dark ones can be worn as whole outfits; the icy ones work best as a single piece against black.

  • True red — clean and deep. The red that makes your skin look clear.
  • Emerald and pine — clear greens with blue underneath. Pine is the darker, richer one and can serve as a near-neutral.
  • Royal blue and sapphire — saturated, deep blues.
  • Aubergine and deep purple — the purple end of your palette, deep and cool.
  • Cool burgundy — a blue-based wine, not a brown one. Near-neutral for you.
  • Icy pink, icy blue and icy lilac — the pale end: white with a drop of pigment, worn crisp against your darks.

Colors to be careful with

Deep Winter has two failure modes: warmth, which turns the skin sallow, and softness, which makes the whole face look tired. Most of the colors below do one or the other.

  • Camel, tan and warm beige — the classic Deep Winter mistake, usually in a coat. Swap for charcoal, black or pewter.
  • Dusty pastels — powder blue, dusty pink, lavender-grey. They wash out against your depth. Swap for the icy version of the same hue, or drop to a jewel tone.
  • Olive and khaki — warm and muted. Swap for pine or emerald.
  • Rust and terracotta — orange-based and earthy. Swap for true red or cool burgundy.
  • Mustard and gold-yellow — sallow-making. Swap for icy lemon in small doses, or for white.
  • Mid-tone greyed blues and marl — soft and middling. Swap for sapphire or ink navy.

Metals, denim, black and white

Silver, platinum and white gold. Yellow gold usually reads slightly dirty against cool skin.

Denim. The darkest indigo you can find, raw or rinsed, and black denim. White denim works against a dark top. A grey-black wash is fine; a pale, faded or yellow-cast wash reads tired on you.

Black. Unreservedly. You are the season for whom black is not just safe but flattering, and a head-to-toe black outfit looks intentional and complete on you. If you want variety, change the texture rather than the color: leather, wool, satin, matte cotton.

White. Pure white, worn crisp. Cream and ivory look yellow next to your skin and dull the contrast that is your best asset. If you want a softer light, use icy grey rather than off-white.

Hair, makeup and glasses notes

Hair. Your hair is your depth, and lightening it more than a shade or two dissolves the season. If you color, keep it dark and cool: blue-black, espresso, cool dark chestnut. Warm caramel or honey highlights push your skin toward yellow. When grey arrives, a clean silver or a cool salt-and-pepper suits you better than a warm blend.

Makeup. Lips take deep and clean colors well: true red, cool berry, deep plum, wine. Blush should be a cool pink or berry rather than peach. Black liner and mascara suit the contrast; a defined brow helps the depth read as intentional. Very pale nude lips can make you look washed out.

Glasses. Black, dark cool tortoise, gunmetal, deep navy and burgundy frames all sit well. Honey tortoise, gold and light warm frames fight your skin.

A week of color combinations

Five pairings for a week. Each keeps something dark and something clean in play.

  • Monday — black trousers, white shirt, true red at the lips or the scarf.
  • Tuesday — deep navy with emerald: a navy coat, an emerald knit.
  • Wednesday — charcoal with icy pink, a small piece of pink against a lot of grey.
  • Thursday — black with cool burgundy, near-monochrome and very rich.
  • Friday — pine green and white, black shoes and silver.

Most often confused with Deep Autumn

Both are deep and dramatic. Deep Winter is cool: black works, camel does not. If warm beige drains you and pure white does not, you are on the winter side.

Neighboring seasons and how to tell

Deep Winter has one neighbor in its own family and one across the warm-cool line. Both are dark, so darkness will not separate them.

True Winter — Cooler and clearer than you, and less reliant on depth. A True Winter can wear icy pastels as whole pieces and looks equally good in black and white; a Deep Winter looks best when the dark end of the palette is doing most of the work.

Deep Autumn — Just as dark, but warm. A Deep Autumn's darks have brown and bronze inside them and a Deep Autumn looks alive in chocolate and camel; a Deep Winter looks drained by both. If black looks like it belongs on you and chocolate looks like a compromise, you are on the winter side.

A three-question self-check

Do these in daylight, with a bare face if you can.

  • Hold black and dark chocolate against your face. If black is clearly better, you are a winter.
  • Hold pure white and cream. If white is clean and cream is dingy, you are a winter.
  • Wear an icy pastel as a whole top. If it looks crisp, you may be a True Winter; if it looks thin, stay here.

Two out of three pointing the same way is enough to start shopping with.

Shopping notes

Search terms surface your colors faster than brand names, and they work in most shops' filters.

  • For neutrals: jet black, optic white, ink navy, charcoal, pewter, espresso.
  • For accents: true red, emerald, pine green, royal blue, sapphire, aubergine, burgundy, ice pink, ice blue.
  • Words that usually mean a color is wrong for you: camel, tan, rust, olive, mustard, dusty, powder, heather.
  • Fabric: depth shows best in fabrics with some density, such as wool, leather, velvet, sateen and heavy jersey; very sheer or fuzzy fabrics lighten and soften a color away from you.

When choosing between two darks online, pick the one that looks almost black in the photo. It is usually the one that looks rich in person.

Questions people ask

Can a Deep Winter wear brown?

The darkest, coolest brown, close to black-brown, worn with white or a bright, is fine. Mid-browns, camel and tan are not. If you want the softness of brown, deep navy or charcoal will give it to you without the warmth.

I look good in black but the quiz said Deep Autumn. Which is right?

Dark seasons are the easiest to confuse. The tiebreaker is undertone: hold pure white and cream against your jaw in daylight. Winter skin looks clean beside white and dingy beside cream; autumn skin does the opposite.

Are pastels off the table?

Powdery, dusty pastels are. Icy ones, which are essentially white with a drop of color, can work as a shirt or a scarf against black. If in doubt, choose the jewel tone in the same hue.

What metals suit Deep Winter?

Silver, platinum, white gold, gunmetal and black metal. A very deep, dark stone such as onyx or a deep garnet also suits you. Yellow gold reads warm and slightly dull on you.
