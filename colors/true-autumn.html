Color season

True Autumn

Warm and rich, with medium-deep, earthy coloring.

True Autumn is the season that looks right in a rust jumper on an October afternoon. Your hair is warm, whether auburn, copper, golden brown or a dark brown with red in it; your eyes are often hazel, warm brown or a mossy green; and your skin has a golden or peachy cast that looks healthiest beside earth colors.

You are the center of the autumn family, warm without being dark, rich without being muted. If a grey T-shirt makes you look tired and a mustard one makes you look well, you are probably here. This page gives you the palette, the neutrals, the colors to be careful with, and answers to the questions about black, white and denim that come up most.

The True Autumn palette

Six colors that carry this season. They are a direction, not a uniform — the point is the family they belong to, not these exact swatches.

What defines it

UndertoneWarm
DepthDeep
ClarityMedium
ContrastMedium

Moderate contrast. You can put a lighter piece against a darker one, but the jump should be a step, not a cliff.

The three dials

Every season is a position on three dials. Hue is undertone: does a color lean toward gold or toward blue. Yours sits firmly at the gold end, the warmest position in the system alongside True Spring. Every color that suits you has yellow, orange or brown inside it, and colors with blue inside them, even dark ones, look borrowed.

Value is depth. True Autumn sits at medium to deep: darker than a Soft Autumn, lighter than a Deep Autumn. You can wear mid-tone earth colors as whole outfits without looking washed out, and you do not need the darkest browns to look finished.

Chroma is clarity. Yours is medium, and this is the dial people misread. True Autumn colors are rich and saturated, but they are the saturation of spices and leaves, not of paint. A pure orange is too sharp; a dusty peach is too soft; a rust is exactly right. The fourth reading, contrast, is medium: a lighter piece against a darker one works, but a stark light-dark split looks harsher than you are.

Colors that work

Warm and earthy at full depth — rust, olive, mustard, brick, deep teal.

Colors that fight it

Cool pastels, pure white, anything icy.

Your best neutrals

Earthy neutrals: camel, olive, chocolate, warm taupe, oatmeal. These are your blacks — an autumn wardrobe built on brown reads more expensive than one built on black.

  • Chocolate — the darkest neutral you need. Warm, rich, and better on you than black.
  • Olive — a green that behaves like a neutral. Olive trousers or an olive jacket go with everything you own.
  • Camel — the mid neutral for coats, knits and bags.
  • Warm khaki and tobacco — the browns between camel and chocolate. Good for trousers.
  • Cream — your white. Warm, soft, and never optic.
  • Deep teal — a warm blue-green that stands in for navy.

Your best accents

Your accents are the spice rack and the forest floor. They work as whole outfits and they look best against olive, chocolate and cream.

  • Rust — the signature color. A red-orange with brown in it.
  • Mustard — deep, warm yellow. Where a Winter wears icy lemon, you wear this.
  • Brick and tomato red — reds with orange underneath, never blue.
  • Moss and forest green — yellow-based greens, mid to deep.
  • Pumpkin and terracotta — the oranges of your family, worn confidently.
  • Warm turquoise and teal — your blues, always with green in them.

Colors to be careful with

True Autumn is let down by coolness, by iciness and by anything too pure. The swaps below keep the hue and fix the dial.

  • Pure optic white — flattens the face and makes the skin look yellow in the wrong way. Swap for cream or oatmeal.
  • Black — hollows the face and makes hair look flat. Swap for chocolate or dark olive.
  • Cool pastels — powder blue, lavender, baby pink. Swap for peach, apricot or cream.
  • Fuchsia and hot pink — blue-based pinks read harsh. Swap for a warm coral-rose or brick.
  • Cool grey — cold and draining. Swap for warm taupe, khaki or olive.
  • Neon and acid brights — too sharp for a medium-clarity season. Swap for the spice version: pumpkin rather than orange, mustard rather than yellow.

Metals, denim, black and white

Gold, brass, bronze and copper. Silver tends to look grey and flat against warm skin.

Denim. Warm indigo, dark or mid, with a slightly brown or green cast rather than a grey one. Olive, tobacco and rust-colored denim are also yours. Very pale, faded or grey-washed denim looks tired next to warm skin.

Black. Not near the face. Black trousers and black boots are fine because they sit far from your skin, but a black top or coat collar drains you. Chocolate, dark olive and deep teal all do the job of black without the cost.

White. Cream, ivory and oatmeal. Optic white is the single most common True Autumn mistake, usually in a shirt or trainers. If you want a light layer near the face, make it cream.

Hair, makeup and glasses notes

Hair. Your hair is your warmth. Copper, auburn, golden brown and warm chestnut all sit naturally, and if you color, go toward those rather than toward ash or cool black. Golden highlights suit; icy or platinum ones fight your skin. Warm, honeyed greys and a copper glaze suit you when grey arrives.

Makeup. Lips take brick, terracotta, warm brown-red and a soft rust. Blush should be a warm peach-brown or terracotta. Brown and bronze eyeliner and a warm brown mascara suit you better than black. Cool pink and mauve lips make the face look grey.

Glasses. Tortoiseshell in its warm honey-and-brown form, bronze, olive, amber and matte gold. Black frames are heavy on you; silver and clear frames look cold.

A week of color combinations

Five pairings for a week. Each keeps a warm mid-tone near the face and the contrast moderate.

  • Monday — olive trousers, cream shirt, rust knit.
  • Tuesday — chocolate with mustard, in roughly equal parts.
  • Wednesday — deep teal with camel, gold at the ears.
  • Thursday — brick red with khaki, brown boots.
  • Friday — moss green with tobacco, and a cream scarf.

Most often confused with Deep Autumn

Same warmth, different depth. True Autumn sits at medium — rust and mustard. Deep Autumn needs chocolate and burgundy, and medium shades start to look washed out on it.

Neighboring seasons and how to tell

True Autumn has neighbors on both sides in its own family, and both are warm, so warmth will not tell them apart. Depth and clarity will.

Soft Autumn — Warm but softer and more blended than you. A Soft Autumn looks best when the colors are greyed a step: camel rather than mustard, salmon rather than rust. If full-strength rust looks too strong on you, look here.

Deep Autumn — Warm but darker than you. A Deep Autumn needs chocolate and burgundy to look finished and finds mid-tone rust slightly washed out. If the darkest browns make you look richer than mid-tone earth colors do, look here.

A three-question self-check

Do these in daylight, with a bare face if you can.

  • Wear rust as a whole top. If it looks like you, you are a True Autumn. If it looks too loud, try Soft Autumn; if it looks faded, try Deep Autumn.
  • Hold cream and pure white against your jaw. If cream is clean and white is harsh, you are on the warm side.
  • Hold camel and chocolate. If camel looks complete and chocolate looks heavy, stay here; if chocolate makes you look richer, try Deep Autumn.

Two out of three pointing the same way is enough to start shopping with.

Shopping notes

Search terms surface your colors faster than brand names, and they work in most shops' filters.

  • For neutrals: chocolate, olive, camel, khaki, tobacco, cream, oatmeal, teal.
  • For accents: rust, mustard, brick red, tomato, terracotta, pumpkin, moss green, forest green.
  • Words that usually mean a color is wrong for you: ice, icy, optic white, jet black, cool grey, fuchsia, neon, pastel.
  • Fabric: warmth shows best in texture, such as suede, corduroy, brushed cotton, tweed, waxed cotton and chunky knits; very shiny surfaces sharpen a color past your clarity.

If a product photo makes you choose between the brown and the black, the brown is almost always your answer.

Questions people ask

Can a True Autumn wear black?

Below the waist and away from the face, yes. As a top, coat collar or scarf, it drains you. Chocolate and dark olive do the job of black on you and look richer doing it.

What about navy?

A warm, slightly greened navy can pass. A cool, inky navy behaves like black on you. Deep teal is the better dark blue for your palette and is worth searching for by name.

Is True Autumn a dark season?

Medium to deep, not dark. You can wear mid-tone earth colors as whole outfits without looking washed out, which a Deep Autumn cannot, and you do not need the darkest browns to look finished.

Which metals?

Gold, brass, bronze and copper. Antique and brushed finishes suit you better than very bright polished gold. Silver looks cold and slightly grey against your skin.
