Color season

Light Summer

Cool and light, with soft, powdery coloring.

Light Summer is the season of the pale, cool morning: powder blue, lilac, the pink of the inside of a shell. Your hair is light and cool, whether ash blonde, light ash brown or a blonde that was much paler in childhood; your eyes are pale, often blue, grey or a soft green; and your skin is fair with a pink or neutral cast that burns before it tans.

If a black coat makes you look like a child wearing an adult's clothes, and a soft blue one makes you look as though you slept well, you are here. This page gives you the palette, the neutrals, the colors to be careful with, and honest answers about black, white and denim for a season that is easily overpowered.

The Light Summer palette

Six colors that carry this season. They are a direction, not a uniform — the point is the family they belong to, not these exact swatches.

What defines it

UndertoneCool
DepthLight
ClarityMuted
ContrastLow

Keep the values close. A low-contrast outfit — one family of tones head to toe — reads as intentional on you, while a hard light-against-dark split looks like the clothes are wearing you.

The three dials

Every season is a position on three dials. Hue is undertone: does a color lean toward gold or toward blue. Yours leans cool, though not as far as a True Summer, which is why a very pale, cool-leaning aqua or a soft sage does not hurt you. Warm and golden colors do: peach and cream turn your skin sallow.

Value is depth, and it is your defining dial. Light Summer sits at the light end of the whole system. Your hair, skin and eyes are all pale, and a color that is much darker than any of them draws the eye away from your face and toward the clothes.

Chroma is clarity. Yours is muted, gently: your colors look powdery rather than greyed, like chalk pastels rather than paint. A pure icy blue is slightly too sharp; a heavily greyed slate is too heavy; a powder blue is exactly right. The fourth reading, contrast, is low: keep the values close and let the whole outfit sit in the light-to-mid range.

Colors that work

Cool and powdery — soft blue, lilac, rose, sage. Keep the contrast low.

Colors that fight it

Black, hot brights, and orange-based warms.

Your best neutrals

Cool soft neutrals: soft navy, greyed taupe, oyster, cocoa with a rose cast, and a grey that leans blue rather than yellow. Soft white beats stark white.

  • Soft white — your best neutral. Cooler than cream, softer than optic white.
  • Dove grey and light grey — the everyday neutrals for knits, trousers and coats.
  • Light greyed navy — the darkest you need, and a soft, dusty one rather than ink.
  • Rose-beige — a beige with pink in it rather than yellow. Your alternative to camel.
  • Light taupe and cocoa-rose — the browns of your palette, always pale and pink-cast.
  • Soft blue-grey — a neutral that doubles as a color.

Your best accents

Your accents are the palest cool colors, worn as whole pieces rather than trims so they do not disappear. They look best against soft white, dove grey and rose-beige.

  • Powder blue — the signature. Soft, pale and cool.
  • Lilac and lavender — pale purples with blue in them.
  • Rose pink — a cool, soft pink. Never hot, never peach.
  • Sage and soft mint — pale, cool greens.
  • Periwinkle — the blue-purple between your blues and your lilacs.
  • Soft raspberry — your one deeper accent, for when you want something that reads clearly.

Colors to be careful with

Light Summer is let down by weight first and by warmth second. The swaps below keep the hue and fix the dial.

  • Black — the most common mistake. It swallows the face. Swap for light greyed navy or mid grey.
  • Optic white — too stark; it makes fair skin look pink and blotchy. Swap for soft white.
  • Orange, coral and rust — warm and loud. Swap for rose pink or soft raspberry.
  • Camel and warm beige — they turn the skin yellow. Swap for rose-beige or dove grey.
  • Hot pink and fuchsia — too saturated. Swap for rose.
  • Deep jewel tones — emerald, royal blue, burgundy. Too heavy against light coloring. Swap for the pale version: sage, powder blue, soft raspberry.

Metals, denim, black and white

Silver, platinum and white gold. Yellow gold usually reads slightly dirty against cool skin.

Denim. Light and mid-blue washes with a cool, slightly grey cast are yours, and a pale grey denim also works. Very dark indigo and black denim look heavy; yellowed vintage washes look warm. Light-wash denim is one of the few places you can wear a lot of one color and look completely at ease.

Black. No, and this is one of the seasons where the rule really holds. Black near the face makes you look drained and makes the hair look flat. Light greyed navy, mid grey and soft charcoal all give you a dark to work with. If you must wear black, keep it below the waist and put soft white between it and your face.

White. Soft white, and plenty of it. Optic white is slightly too bright and cream is slightly too warm; the white in between, the white of a washed cotton sheet, is yours.

Hair, makeup and glasses notes

Hair. Your hair is light and cool, and the temptation to add warmth with honey or golden highlights is the one to resist: it turns the skin sallow. Ash blonde, cool beige-blonde, platinum and light ash brown all suit. If your hair has darkened with age, cool highlights that bring it back toward its childhood color usually flatter. When grey arrives, it tends to blend beautifully.

Makeup. Keep it light and cool. Lips take rose, soft berry and a cool pink-nude; blush should be a soft cool pink. Grey and soft navy eyeliner suit you better than black, and brown-black or brown mascara is gentler than a hard black. Heavy bronzer and warm contour are the things to avoid.

Glasses. Silver, light grey, clear, pale pink, soft blue and a light greyed tortoiseshell all sit well. Black frames dominate a light face; gold and honey tortoise look warm and heavy.

A week of color combinations

Five pairings for a week. Each keeps the values light and close together.

  • Monday — light grey trousers, soft white shirt, powder blue knit.
  • Tuesday — lilac with dove grey, silver at the ears.
  • Wednesday — rose pink with rose-beige, in roughly equal parts.
  • Thursday — sage with soft white, and light-wash denim.
  • Friday — periwinkle with light greyed navy, grey shoes.

Most often confused with Light Spring

Same lightness, opposite undertone. If gold jewellery looks slightly dirty on you and silver looks clean, you are in Light Summer.

Neighboring seasons and how to tell

Light Summer has one neighbor in its own family and one across the warm-cool line. Both are light, so lightness will not tell them apart.

True Summer — Cool but deeper than you. A True Summer can wear a mid-depth plum, teal or soft navy as a whole piece and look finished; a Light Summer looks slightly weighed down by the same colors. If the mid-tones look like you and the pale ones look faint, look here.

Light Spring — Just as light, but warm. A Light Spring glows in peach, apricot and cream and looks sallow in cool pink; a Light Summer is the reverse. If gold looks slightly dirty on you and silver looks clean, you are a summer.

A three-question self-check

Do these in daylight, with a bare face if you can.

  • Hold peach and rose pink against your face. If rose wins, you are on the cool side.
  • Hold soft white and cream. If soft white looks fresh and cream looks yellow, you are a summer.
  • Wear a mid-depth plum as a whole top. If it looks heavy, stay here; if it looks like you, try True Summer.

Two out of three pointing the same way is enough to start shopping with.

Shopping notes

Search terms surface your colors faster than brand names, and they work in most shops' filters.

  • For neutrals: soft white, dove grey, light grey, rose-beige, blue-grey, light navy.
  • For accents: powder blue, lilac, lavender, rose, sage, mint, periwinkle, raspberry.
  • Words that usually mean a color is wrong for you: jet black, optic white, cream, camel, coral, hot, deep, jewel.
  • Fabric: lightness shows best in fine, soft fabrics such as cotton voile, fine knit, washed linen, chambray and soft jersey; heavy, dense or very shiny fabrics add weight a light season does not have.

The words pale, powder, pastel and light in a listing are usually good news for you, provided the base color is cool.

Questions people ask

Is Light Summer just pastels?

The palette is pale, but it is not only pastel. A soft raspberry, a light greyed navy and a mid grey give you depth when you need it. The rule is cool and light, not cool and pale.

Can I wear black to a formal event?

Choose light greyed navy, soft charcoal or a mid grey instead; they read just as formal and keep your face alive. If black is required, wear it below the waist and put soft white or powder blue at the neck.

Gold or silver?

Silver, platinum, white gold and a pale, cool rose gold. Yellow gold looks slightly dirty against cool, fair skin. Small, delicate pieces suit the scale of your coloring.

What if the quiz said Light Spring?

The two are neighbors and the difference is undertone only. Do the peach-against-rose test above in daylight. Whichever makes your skin look alive rather than sallow is your side of the line.
