Kibbe body type

Soft Natural

Broad frame, softened edges.

One outfit in Soft Natural lines for each wardrobe — a starting idea, not a rule.

You share Natural's relaxed, broad-boned foundation, but with extra softness in the flesh and features that takes the edge off. Look for clothes that stay easy and unstructured like Natural's, but choose fabrics with a bit of weight and drape rather than anything stiff or crisp.

What the lines actually mean

Your frame has the Natural family’s width and bluntness, softened at every edge. The shoulder is broad and fairly straight, the jaw and joints read as blunt rather than pointed, and the vertical line is moderate to a little short. Over that frame the flesh is soft and rounded: a real waist, hips and thighs that are present, a soft upper arm, a full cheek. That combination is why pure Natural advice, which keeps everything straight and unfitted, tends to make you look wider and heavier than you are, and why pure Romantic advice, which fits everything and adds curve, tends to look overdone. What works sits between them. Relaxed shape, because the frame is broad. A gently marked waist, because the waist is there. Fabrics that are soft and slightly textured, because both stiffness and shine sit badly against rounded flesh on broad bones. A wrap dress in jersey is the perfect example: it is easy, it is textured, and it holds the waist without pulling anything tight. The words to keep in your head are relaxed and soft, in that order.

What works on a Soft Natural

  • Relaxed shapes with a gently defined waist: a wrap dress in a soft fabric, a belted cardigan.
  • Soft, slightly textured fabrics — brushed cotton, jersey, fine knit, washed silk.
  • Rounded, unfussy details: a scoop neck, a soft blouse, a slightly gathered skirt.
  • Natural, mid-scale accessories with a little softness — a rounded bag, a hoop earring.

What fights your lines

  • Rigid tailoring and sharp shoulders overwhelm the softness.
  • Heavy embellishment and very ornate prints.
  • Anything that hides the waist entirely on a frame that has one.

A soft wrap dress in rust jersey with tan flat sandals.

The five pieces to start with

A starter capsule in Soft Natural lines. Each one is chosen because it does a job the others cannot, and because all five work together in any combination.

A wrap dress in jersey or brushed cotton

It gives you an easy shape and a waist in one piece. Aim for a wrap that crosses at your natural waist and a skirt with a little swing.

A soft, drapey cardigan you can belt

Long enough to cover the hip, in a knit with movement. Belted at the waist it is an outfit; open, it is a layer.

A washed-silk or soft cotton blouse

Slightly loose through the body, tucked at the front. A scoop or a soft V at the neck; no stiff collar.

Straight or gently tapered jeans with a mid to high rise

The rise acknowledges the waist; the straight leg skims the hip and thigh rather than clinging.

A rounded shoulder bag in soft leather

Mid-scale, unstructured, in a matte finish. A hard boxy bag looks like a mismatch against the softness.

Necklines, hemlines and where the waist sits

Necklines. Soft and open: a scoop, a soft V, a wrap neckline, a wide rounded crew, a gently draped cowl. Sharp collars and high stiff necks look severe against a soft face and a broad shoulder. Off-the-shoulder works when the fabric is soft.

Hemlines. Moderate: knee to midi for skirts and dresses, with a little movement in the skirt; full length or ankle for trousers. A very short hem looks abrupt on a broad frame, and a stiff long one looks heavy. A fluid midi is usually the safest length you own.

The waist. At the natural waist, gently. A wrap, a soft belt, a knit that skims in at the waist, a cardigan belted loosely. The waist is real, so ignoring it entirely makes the frame read as a rectangle; pulling it in hard with a stiff belt looks constricted. Soft definition is the whole point of the type.

A week of outfits

Five days, five situations, all built from the same logic. Swap colors for your own season; keep the shapes.

  • Work. A washed-silk blouse in a muted tone front-tucked into straight trousers with a soft drape, a long cardigan, rounded loafers, a hoop earring.
  • Weekend. A soft knit top, straight jeans with a mid rise, a suede jacket with a rounded shoulder, low-profile sneakers, a slouchy crossbody.
  • Evening. A wrap dress in jersey in a deep color, a soft belt at the waist, a low block heel, a rounded clutch.
  • Cold day. A soft wool coat belted loosely at the waist, a fine knit dress with a scoop neck, tall boots with a soft shaft, a chunky knit scarf.
  • Warm day. A gathered midi skirt in soft cotton with a fitted rib tank, flat leather sandals, a straw shoulder bag.

Commonly confused with

Flamboyant Natural — both are broad and relaxed, but Flamboyant Natural is longer and wants no waist emphasis; Soft Natural wants a little.

Flamboyant Natural (read the guide)

Flamboyant Natural has your broad frame with a long vertical and straight flesh. It wants no waist emphasis and full-length everything. If cinching the waist looks like it is creating a shape rather than showing one, and a long open coat looks better than a belted one, read the Flamboyant Natural guide.

Soft Classic (read the guide)

Soft Classic is also soft and waisted, but on a moderate, symmetrical frame that wants polish and precision. If a tailored sheath with a rounded lapel looks better on you than a relaxed wrap dress, and a boxy jacket looks untidy rather than easy, you may be a Soft Classic.

A three-question self-check
  1. Does a gently belted soft dress look like your best self, or like an unnecessary cinch?
  2. Does a relaxed, unlined jacket look easy on you, or does it look untidy?
  3. Does a stiff, structured shift dress look sleek, or does it look like a box?

Best self, easy, and box: Soft Natural. Unnecessary cinch on the first points to Flamboyant Natural; untidy on the second points to Soft Classic.

Shopping notes

Search for: “wrap dress”, “jersey”, “belted cardigan”, “scoop neck”, “soft blouse”, “washed silk”, “relaxed straight”, “suede jacket”, “hoop earring”, “midi skirt”.

Scroll past: “structured”, “boxy”, “padded shoulder”, “bodycon”, “embellished”, “stiff”, “crisp shirt collar”, “mini”, “column”.

Try the same wrap dress across two sizes: the smaller will hold the waist better and the larger will sit better on the shoulder, and which one wins tells you where your frame leads. Fabric composition matters more than usual for you; a wrap in stiff cotton and the same wrap in jersey are two different dresses.

Questions people ask about the Soft Natural type

Am I a Soft Natural or just a curvy Natural?

The type already includes the curve. A pure Natural has straighter flesh and can go without a waist; you cannot, at least not without looking wider. If a soft waist consistently makes your outfits better, you are in the right place.

Can I wear tailoring?

Soft tailoring, yes. A jacket with a rounded, unpadded shoulder and a fabric that drapes, worn open or belted loosely. Sharp lapels, padded shoulders and stiff suiting are the versions to skip.

What about prints?

Mid-scale, soft-edged and a little loose: a watercolor floral, a soft paisley, an irregular stripe. Very small neat prints and very large hard-edged ones both sit oddly on this frame.

What does this look like in menswear?

An unstructured jacket with a natural shoulder, a soft knit or a washed cotton shirt worn open at the collar, a straight trouser in a fabric with drape, a suede or nubuck shoe. The line is easy, and the fabrics are the soft end of everything.
