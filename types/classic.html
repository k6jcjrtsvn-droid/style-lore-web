Kibbe body type

Classic

Balanced, clean, symmetrical.

One outfit in Classic lines for each wardrobe — a starting idea, not a rule.

Your proportions sit in true balance — no strong curve or sharp angle dominates. Tailored, well-proportioned clothing with clean lines and moderate detail will always look intentional on you; the goal is polish and symmetry rather than a strong directional statement.

What the lines actually mean

Your frame is in balance. The vertical line is moderate, so you read as neither tall nor short. The bones are moderate, neither broad nor delicate, and the shoulder, jaw and joints are neither sharp nor round but somewhere even between the two. The flesh is moderate too: a waist that is there without being dramatic, limbs that are in proportion, features that are symmetrical and evenly sized. It is the only frame in the system where nothing is exaggerated in any direction, and that is exactly what makes it tricky to dress, because most fashion is built on exaggeration. Oversized, ultra-fitted, very long, very cropped, very ornate and very undone all look like a costume on you, not because you cannot carry them but because they add something that is not there. What looks right is moderation done well: a well-cut blazer, a knee-length skirt, a sheath dress, a straight trouser, smooth refined fabrics in clean colors. The details should be timeless rather than trend-led, and the whole outfit should read as symmetrical. Polish is the word. On you, it is not a compromise; it is the line.

What works on a Classic

  • Balanced, symmetrical, moderate everything: a well-cut blazer, a knee-length skirt, a simple sheath.
  • Smooth, refined fabrics — fine wool, silk blends, quality cotton — in clean solids or subtle patterns.
  • Timeless details: a notched collar, a straight hem, a neat cuff.
  • Understated, medium-scale accessories: pearl studs, a leather loafer, a classic tote.

What fights your lines

  • Anything extreme — oversized, ultra-fitted, very ornate or very undone.
  • Loud prints and heavy texture that disrupt the smoothness.
  • Trend-driven pieces with exaggerated proportions.

The five pieces to start with

A starter capsule in Classic lines. Each one is chosen because it does a job the others cannot, and because all five work together in any combination.

A well-cut blazer in a smooth fabric

Moderate length, moderate lapel, a natural shoulder with the lightest structure. It should fit exactly and be no more remarkable than that.

A sheath dress to the knee

One clean shape that skims the body, in crepe or fine wool. It is the most Classic garment there is, and it goes from work to dinner with a change of shoe.

Straight trousers with a clean line

Mid rise, straight from the hip, ending just at the shoe. No exaggerated width, no skinny taper.

A silk or fine-cotton shell

A simple sleeveless or short-sleeved top with a clean neckline, to wear under the blazer and tuck into the skirt.

A leather loafer or a moderate-heel pump

Medium toe, medium heel, good leather. The shoe should complete the outfit, not comment on it.

Necklines, hemlines and where the waist sits

Necklines. Moderate and clean: a notched collar, a modest V, a jewel neck, a boat neck, a small shirt collar. Very deep, very high, very wide or very ruffled necklines are all exaggerations. A simple neckline with a single pendant or pearl studs is the template.

Hemlines. At the knee for skirts and dresses, or a straight midi; at the shoe for trousers; at the hip for jackets and the knee for coats. Minis and maxis both move you out of balance. Straight hems rather than curved, asymmetric or tiered.

The waist. At the natural waist, acknowledged without emphasis. A sheath that skims in, a slim belt, a blazer with a gentle shape. Nothing cinched, nothing dropped. The waist is one point in a symmetrical line, and it should look like the clothes simply fit.

A week of outfits

Five days, five situations, all built from the same logic. Swap colors for your own season; keep the shapes.

  • Work. A gray blazer, a silk shell, straight trousers, leather loafers, a slim watch and pearl studs.
  • Weekend. A fine-knit crew-neck in navy, straight mid-blue jeans, a trench, white leather sneakers with a clean toe, a classic tote.
  • Evening. A camel or black sheath to the knee, a moderate-heel pump, a single pendant, a small structured bag.
  • Cold day. A single-breasted wool coat to the knee, a cashmere turtleneck, straight wool trousers, ankle boots with a moderate heel, a plain scarf.
  • Warm day. A cotton shirt dress to the knee with a slim belt, flat leather sandals with a simple strap, a straw tote.

Commonly confused with

Soft Classic — adds a little curve and softness; Dramatic Classic adds a little edge. Pure Classic sits right in the middle.

Soft Classic (read the guide)

Soft Classic has your balance with a little more roundness in the flesh and features. It wants a rounded lapel, a softer fabric and a slightly more defined waist. If a crisp straight sheath looks a little severe and a soft draped one looks right, read the Soft Classic guide.

Dramatic Classic (read the guide)

Dramatic Classic has your balance with a touch of sharpness. It wants a squarer shoulder and a crisper collar. If a sharp-lapel blazer looks better on you than a moderate one, and soft details look slightly limp, you may be a Dramatic Classic.

A three-question self-check
  1. Does a moderate, unremarkable blazer that fits perfectly look like your best outfit, or does it feel like it needs something?
  2. Does a very sharp, squared shoulder look polished on you, or a touch hard?
  3. Does a crisp, straight sheath look right on you, or a little severe, so that a softer draped version looks better?

Best outfit, a touch hard, and right: Classic. Polished on the second points to Dramatic Classic; severe on the third points to Soft Classic.

Regular ranges fit you more often than they fit anyone else, which makes fit the only real work: sleeves to the wrist bone, trousers to the shoe, a blazer that closes without pulling. Choose good fabric over interesting detail every time; on this frame the fabric is the detail.

Questions people ask about the Classic type

Is Classic just boring?

Only if you confuse restraint with absence. A Classic in a perfectly cut camel coat over a black sheath is not boring; it is exact. Where other frames rely on drama or softness or contrast, yours relies on proportion, and proportion done well reads as quiet confidence.

Can I wear trends?

Yes, in moderate versions. If the trend is a wide trouser, wear the moderate-wide one. If it is a cropped jacket, wear the one that hits the hip rather than the ribcage. Take the idea and remove the exaggeration.

What about color and pattern?

Clean solids, subtle stripes, small checks, a quiet houndstooth. Two or three colors in an outfit rather than one loud print. Your color season decides the shades; your type asks that they be used simply.

What does this look like in menswear?

A two-button suit in a fine wool with a moderate lapel, a shirt with a standard collar, trousers with a clean break, a plain leather oxford or loafer. Nothing oversized, nothing skinny, nothing distressed.
