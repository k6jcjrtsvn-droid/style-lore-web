Kibbe body type

Natural

Blunt, broad, unfussy.

One outfit in Natural lines for each wardrobe — a starting idea, not a rule.

Your bone structure and features read broad and blunt rather than sharp or curved — a genuinely relaxed, athletic line. The most flattering clothes are similarly unstructured: soft tailoring, natural fibers, easy movement, and nothing that fights the body with tight seaming or delicate detail.

What the lines actually mean

Your frame is defined by width and bluntness more than by length or curve. The shoulder is broad and straight, the jaw is square, the hands are wide, and the joints read as blunt rather than pointed or delicate. The vertical line is moderate, neither long nor short, and the flesh is straight and firm rather than round, so there is a little waist but not a dramatic one. The whole frame reads athletic and easy, and it is in a sense the most forgiving frame there is, because it does not need much. What it does need is for the clothes not to argue with it. Sharp, padded tailoring argues with the width of the shoulder; tight seaming argues with the straightness of the flesh; ornate detail argues with the bluntness. What works is clean, relaxed shape and honest texture. A straight coat, a boxy jacket, a simple shift, a shirt that is not tucked too tightly. Matte fabrics with a surface to them: cotton, linen, wool, suede. Nothing needs to be exaggerated, and nothing needs to be small. The aim is the outfit looking like it was found rather than assembled.

What works on a Natural

  • Clean, easy shapes with a relaxed fit: a straight coat, a boxy jacket, a simple shift.
  • Matte, textured fabrics — cotton, wool, linen, suede — in earthy or muted tones.
  • Minimal ornamentation; let the texture and the cut do the talking.
  • Practical, mid-scale accessories: a leather satchel, a plain belt, a wooden bangle.

What fights your lines

  • Stiff, sharp tailoring with exaggerated shoulders.
  • Ornate detail — ruffles, sequins, lace — reads fussy against blunt, broad lines.
  • Very fitted, body-conscious silhouettes that fight the width of the frame.

The five pieces to start with

A starter capsule in Natural lines. Each one is chosen because it does a job the others cannot, and because all five work together in any combination.

A straight, unlined jacket or a boxy denim jacket

No shoulder pad, a straight hem, an easy fit through the body. The shoulder should sit on yours without pulling.

A relaxed straight or wide-leg trouser

Mid to high rise, straight from the hip, ending at the ankle or the top of the shoe. Chinos, wide jeans, soft wool.

A simple shift or a rib-knit dress

One clean shape, skimming rather than fitted, in a fabric with texture. It takes a jacket or a cardigan over the top without fuss.

A cotton or linen shirt

Worn half-tucked, sleeves rolled, top button open. It is the natural centerpiece of most of your outfits.

A flat shoe with substance: a loafer, a leather sneaker, a flat boot

Practical scale, matte finish. A delicate strappy shoe looks precarious under a broad frame.

Necklines, hemlines and where the waist sits

Necklines. Easy and open: a V, a wide crew, an unbuttoned shirt collar, a boat neck, a loose funnel. A high tight neck narrows a broad shoulder in the wrong direction; a ruffled or bowed neckline looks like it belongs to someone with softer lines.

Hemlines. Moderate to long. Trousers to the ankle bone or the top of the shoe; skirts at the knee to midi; coats to the knee or a little below. Short and tight is the combination to avoid. A cropped wide trouser works because it is relaxed; a cropped fitted one does not.

The waist. Suggested, not cinched. A half-tuck, a belt on the hip, a jacket left open, a dress that skims the waist without a seam. The frame has a waist, but pulling it in hard creates an hourglass that is not there and makes the shoulders look wider. Straight lines that lightly acknowledge the waist are the target.

A week of outfits

Five days, five situations, all built from the same logic. Swap colors for your own season; keep the shapes.

  • Work. A boxy navy unlined jacket over a white cotton shirt, straight camel trousers, leather loafers, a plain belt.
  • Weekend. A denim jacket, a plain gray tee, wide cropped chinos, white leather sneakers, a leather satchel.
  • Evening. A rib-knit column dress in a deep matte color, a suede jacket over the shoulders, flat leather sandals, a wooden bangle.
  • Cold day. A straight wool coat worn open, a felted crew-neck sweater, straight jeans, flat leather boots, a plain wool scarf.
  • Warm day. A linen shirt half-tucked into tapered linen trousers, leather sandals, a straw tote.

Commonly confused with

Soft Natural — the softer sibling adds curve and wants gentle waist definition; pure Natural keeps it straighter.

Soft Natural (read the guide)

Soft Natural has your broad, blunt frame with rounder flesh and a more defined waist. It wants a gentle waist and a softer fabric where you want a straighter line. If a wrap dress in jersey looks better on you than a straight shift, read the Soft Natural guide.

Flamboyant Natural (read the guide)

Flamboyant Natural is your frame with a long vertical added. It needs longer everything and can carry a floor-length coat. If knee-length coats look short on you and ankle-length looks right, you may be a Flamboyant Natural.

A three-question self-check
  1. Does a straight, boxy jacket with no waist look right on you, or does it look shapeless?
  2. Does a knee-length coat look proportionate, or too short?
  3. Does a gathered, ornate detail like a ruffle look charming on you, or slightly silly?

Right, proportionate, and silly: Natural. Shapeless on the first points to Soft Natural; too short on the second points to Flamboyant Natural.

Shopping notes

Scroll past: “padded shoulder”, “bodycon”, “ruffle”, “sequin”, “peplum”, “skinny”, “delicate”, “satin”, “embellished”.

Men's and unisex ranges are worth trying for shirts, jackets and knits; the straight cut and the broad shoulder are often exactly what you need. Buy the size that fits the shoulder and let the body of the garment be a little easy; that ease is the point.

Questions people ask about the Natural type

Is Natural just casual?

No. It is relaxed in line, which is not the same as informal. A Natural at a formal event wears a soft, unpadded suit in a textured fabric, or a long simple dress in matte crepe, with one substantial accessory. Formal without stiffness.

Can I wear a fitted dress?

Skimming, yes; body-conscious, no. A knit dress that follows the body loosely works. A tight bandage dress pulls across the shoulder and hip and looks tense. The difference is whether the fabric touches you or grips you.

What about jewelry?

Mid-scale, matte and organic: a wooden bangle, a leather cord, a hammered metal cuff, a plain hoop. Small delicate chains vanish; large sparkly pieces look out of place.

How does this work in menswear?

An unstructured jacket, a straight chino or a relaxed jean, an oxford or chambray shirt with the collar open, a chunky loafer or a leather sneaker. Skip the sharp suit with the padded shoulder and the skinny trouser.
