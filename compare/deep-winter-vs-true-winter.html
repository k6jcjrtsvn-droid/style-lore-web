Deep Winter vs True Winter

How to tell which one you actually are.

The short answer. Cooler and clearer than you, and less reliant on depth. A True Winter can wear icy pastels as whole pieces and looks equally good in black and white; a Deep Winter looks best when the dark end of the palette is doing most of the work.

What separates them, from each side

Each guide describes the other from its own point of view, and the two tests are different. This is the only page that carries both.

Read from Deep Winter

Cooler and clearer than you, and less reliant on depth. A True Winter can wear icy pastels as whole pieces and looks equally good in black and white; a Deep Winter looks best when the dark end of the palette is doing most of the work.

Read from True Winter

Shares your cool side and your love of black, but the whole impression is darker and the undertone a touch more neutral, so a Deep Winter can carry a burgundy that would dull you. If icy pink looks thin on you rather than crisp, look here.

Check it on yourself

The self-check from the Deep Winter guide

Do these in daylight, with a bare face if you can.

  • Hold black and dark chocolate against your face. If black is clearly better, you are a winter.
  • Hold pure white and cream. If white is clean and cream is dingy, you are a winter.
  • Wear an icy pastel as a whole top. If it looks crisp, you may be a True Winter; if it looks thin, stay here.
The self-check from the True Winter guide

Do these in daylight, with a bare face if you can.

  • Put pure white and cream against your jaw. If cream looks dingy and white looks clean, you are on the winter side of the wheel.
  • Wear black and white with nothing else. If the outfit looks complete, you are likely a True Winter. If something seems missing, try Bright Winter.
  • Hold icy pink against your face. If it reads crisp, stay here. If it reads washed out, try Deep Winter.
