Theatrical Romantic vs Romantic

How to tell which one you actually are.

The short answer. pure Romantic is fully soft and rounded; Theatrical Romantic carries a sharp edge and can take a pointed toe or a defined shoulder.

Theatrical Romantic

Romantic

What separates them, from each side

Each guide describes the other from its own point of view, and the two tests are different. This is the only page that carries both.

Read from Theatrical Romantic

Pure Romantic is fully soft and rounded with no sharp note anywhere, and it looks best with round toes, soft shoulders and gathered detail. Those same choices look a touch bland on you. If a pointed shoe looks harsh rather than finishing, read the Romantic guide.

Read from Romantic

Theatrical Romantic has your curve with a sharp note in the bones, and it looks better with a pointed shoe or a defined shoulder as an accent. On you those look harsh. If one sharp detail finishes an outfit rather than jarring it, you may be a Theatrical Romantic.

Check it on yourself

The self-check from the Theatrical Romantic guide

Finish, childish, and glamorous: Theatrical Romantic. Harsh on the first points to Romantic; lively on the second points to Soft Gamine.

  • Does a pointed-toe shoe finish an outfit on you, or does it look harsh against the softness?
  • Does a small, crisp collar look lively, or does it look childish?
  • Does a fitted satin dress with a deep V look glamorous, or too much for daytime you?
The self-check from the Romantic guide

Harsh, standing away, and luxurious: Romantic. A pointed toe that looks right points to Theatrical Romantic; a polished blazer on the second points to Soft Classic.

  • Does a rounded-toe shoe look right on you, and a pointed one look harsh?
  • Does a soft, tailored blazer with a rounded lapel look polished, or does it look like it is standing away from you?
  • Does a fully draped, gathered dress look luxurious, or a bit much?
